Where it runs

The facilitator is public, at scrip-production.up.railway.app. Nothing needs installing to see it work: 

curl https://scrip-production.up.railway.app/health
curl https://scrip-production.up.railway.app/price
curl -i https://scrip-production.up.railway.app/api/haiku

The last one answers 402 with a price in USD, the FXRP it converts to, the FTSO rate it was derived from, and a single-use invoice id - priced at the moment you ask.

What has been verified, and how

npm run test:fork forks live Coston2, deploys the facilitator onto the fork, and settles a real invoice against the real FXRP contract - the deployed vendored FAsset, resolved through AssetManagerFXRP.fAsset(), not a mock. Each property below is asserted, and the attacks are run as attacks.

  • The payment is gasless. The payer's native balance is forced to zero before signing and checked to still be zero after. The receipt shows the relayer's address paid.
  • Signatures verify against FXRP's vendored permit - not stock OpenZeppelin, and not a mock.
  • A relayer cannot redirect the payment. A third account calls settle() substituting itself as payee and is rejected with IntentNotSignedByPayer.
  • An invoice settles once. Replaying the same signatures reverts with AlreadySettled.
  • A front-runner cannot grief a payment. A third account submits the permit straight to FXRP, burning the nonce. settle() still delivers in full.

Where the fork test stops short.

The token is real, but the payer's FXRP balance is synthetic - written straight into the token's balance mapping on the fork, because minting FAssets legitimately requires an XRP payment proof that a test cannot produce. So the fork run proves the contract logic and the signing path; it does not prove a payer funded through the normal FAssets route.

What is not done

Concurrent payments from one payer. EIP-2612 nonces are sequential, so an agent paying two endpoints at once will have the second permit fail on a consumed nonce. EIP-3009's random nonces avoid this, but FXRP does not implement EIP-3009. Payments are serialised per payer until that is addressed.

An authenticated facilitator. POST /settle is open, so anyone who can reach it can make its relayer pay gas for a payment to any payee. Permissionless settlement is the contract's design and the reason the PaymentIntent exists, but a hosted facilitator would need a payee allowlist or rate limiting before it faced the open internet.

Invoice ids across a restart. Quotes are signed with SCRIP_INVOICE_SECRET. Without it set, the service picks a random key per process, and a quote issued before a restart is rejected after it.

Roadmap

  1. Concurrent payments from one assistant

    The MCP server can pay for one resource at a time. Paying for two at once means fixing the sequential-nonce limitation above, not just calling the tool twice.

  2. Namespace invoice ids per payer

    settled is keyed on invoiceId alone. Invoice ids travel the HTTP path, so anyone who sees one can settle their own payment against it first and burn it for the intended payer. No funds are at risk - the intent still binds payer, payee and amount - but the service has to reissue.

  3. Mainnet FXRP

    Everything here is Coston2. The signing path is unchanged on mainnet, but the facilitator would need redeploying and auditing against the real asset.
